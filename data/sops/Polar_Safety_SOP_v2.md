# Polar Expedition Safety and Operational Protocol (SOP v2.1)

## Section 1: General Expedition Safety Guidelines

All polar expedition personnel must adhere strictly to environmental safety margins. Extreme cold temperatures (-40°C to -60°C) and sudden coastal blizzards represent immediate threats to life and equipment integrity.

- **Mandatory Safety Window:** No overland transport convoy may depart if wind speeds exceed 35 knots or visibility drops below 50 meters.
- **Station Reserve Thresholds:** All base stations must maintain a minimum of 20% fuel reserve capacity for station heating and primary generator operation.

---

## Section 2: Fuel Supply and Storage Protocols

Fuel management in polar environments requires specialized anti-gel Grade A polar diesel additives.

- **Primary Diesel Generators:** Primary station generators consume approximately 25 liters per hour under baseline electrical loads.
- **Low Temperature Contingency:** In the event of a fuel transport delay exceeding 12 hours:
  1. Non-essential scientific research equipment must be powered down to reduce load.
  2. Auxiliary heat exchangers must be routed to main habitat modules.
  3. Secondary fuel reserves from nearest supply hubs (e.g., Depot Gamma) must be staged for transfer.

---

## Section 3: Emergency Resource Allocation & Triage

During severe weather alerts or critical equipment breakdowns (e.g., station generator turbine failure):

- **Proximity Allocation:** Resource allocation engines must prioritize operational assets located within immediate geographic proximity (<100 km).
- **Search and Rescue (SAR):** Medical trauma kits and thermal survival heaters must remain staged and unassigned for emergency dispatch.
